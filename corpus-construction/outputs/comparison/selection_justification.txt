THE ARGUMENT IN ONE PARAGRAPH
  The unit of analysis is the GAME, not the review. A pattern missed in one review is
  recovered from the next review of the same game; a pattern wrongly asserted is not
  recovered by anything. So the quantity to protect is precision-on-emission, not
  recall. high has the higher precision (0.887 vs 0.864) and emits fewer spurious
  labels (12 vs 16) than xhigh, is statistically indistinguishable from it on every
  quality measure, and costs half as much and half the wall clock. Prompt v3 was built
  specifically to make the model label more and stop returning NONE; it is 24.4% larger
  and it did not do so at any reasoning effort. We therefore keep v2 and treat
  conservative labelling as a stable property of the annotator rather than a defect to
  be prompted away.


====================================================================================================
1. THE UNIT OF ANALYSIS ARGUMENT -- WHY MISSED LABELS ARE CHEAP HERE
====================================================================================================
CORPUS SHAPE
  199,721 reviews over 88 games
  reviews per game:  min 694   p25 2,342   median 2,343   p75 2,344   max 2,347

  The corpus is near-uniform per game. A pattern that genuinely characterises a game
  has ~2,343 independent chances to be observed, not one.

RECOVERY BY AGGREGATION
  If a pattern is mentioned in n of a game's reviews and the annotator detects any one
  mention with probability r, the game-level detection probability is 1 - (1-r)^n.
  Using the measured gold-75 recall of each setting (high r=0.686, xhigh r=0.745):

    n mentions     high      xhigh      gap
             1    0.686      0.745   +0.059
             2    0.901      0.935   +0.034
             3    0.969      0.983   +0.014
             5    0.997      0.999   +0.002
             8    1.000      1.000   +0.000
            20    1.000      1.000   +0.000

  The recall advantage of xhigh is worth 0.059 at a single mention and effectively
  nothing from three mentions onward. At ~2,343 reviews per game, any pattern present
  often enough to be a property OF THE GAME is far past that point.

  ASSUMPTION, STATED: this treats detection failures as independent across reviews.
  That holds for ordinary misses; it does NOT hold for a label the annotator is blind
  to as a class. Section 6 records which labels those are. The assumption is what makes
  the aggregation argument work, so it is checkable, not hidden.

IS high's EXTRA MISS RANDOM OR SYSTEMATIC?  (the test of that assumption)
  high misses 43 gold labels, xhigh misses 35 -- a difference of 8.
  Those 8 are spread over 9 distinct labels, at most ONE extra miss on any single label:

    label                       support   high tp/fn   xhigh tp/fn   high extra miss
    M_EasyToPurchase                  5       3/2          4/1               +1
    M_Gambling                        4       2/2          3/1               +1
    P_RewardMania                    10       7/3          8/2               +1
    S_Competition                    11       9/2         10/1               +1
    S_ForcedFellowship                2       1/1          2/0               +1
    S_FriendSpamImpersonation         2       1/1          2/0               +1
    T_Advertisement                   9       7/2          8/1               +1
    T_Grinding                        5       3/2          4/1               +1
    T_MandatoryMarathon               6       3/3          4/2               +1
    T_InfiniteTreadmill               2       2/0          1/1               -1

  No label loses more than one instance, and one label goes the other way. This is
  scattered noise, not a blind spot -- exactly the error profile that aggregation
  over ~2,343 reviews recovers.

THE DIRECTION THAT DOES NOT RECOVER
  gold-75, teacher_v2_full:
    high   precision 0.887   12 spurious labels   43 missed
    xhigh  precision 0.864   16 spurious labels   35 missed

  high emits FEWER wrong labels in absolute terms. Under a game-level design, a
  spurious label propagates to the game and no amount of extra data removes it,
  whereas a missed label is re-offered by the next review. Choosing on precision
  selects high; choosing on recall selects xhigh. The design says precision.


====================================================================================================
2. WHY high vs xhigh IS A COINFLIP -- ALL CALCULATIONS
====================================================================================================
PAIRED BOOTSTRAP, gold-75, 20,000 resamples, paired by review_id
  (each resample draws 75 reviews with replacement and recomputes both settings on the
   same draw, so the comparison is not contaminated by which reviews were sampled)

    measure                 high    xhigh    delta   P(xhigh better)
    micro-F1               0.774    0.800   +0.027             0.922
    MASI to gold           0.581    0.615   +0.035             0.875
    Jaccard to gold        0.650    0.687   +0.037             0.917
    exact set match        0.453    0.493   +0.040             0.797

  significance threshold  0.95        grey zone  [0.05, 0.95]
  minimum detectable effect at n=75:  0.032     (largest delta observed: 0.040)
  95% CI micro-F1:  high [0.708, 0.831]   xhigh [0.743, 0.851]   -- overlapping

  Not one measure reaches the threshold. Three of the four deltas are at or below the
  MDE. The confidence intervals overlap across most of their width.

THEY ARE SUBSTANTIALLY THE SAME ANNOTATOR
  identical label sets on 58 of 75 reviews
  xhigh closer to gold on 10 reviews, high closer on 7
  agreement between the two settings: MASI 0.800, Jaccard 0.848, exact 0.747
    -- higher than either setting's agreement with ANY human rater (max 0.659)

  Two raters that agree with each other more than either agrees with a human are not
  two different annotators. The 17 reviews where they differ are the entire basis for
  a 0.027 micro-F1 gap.

WHAT high BUYS
    measure                        high      xhigh
    projected cost at 200k         $407       $839
    wall clock at 11 workers      ~58 h     ~129 h
    output tokens per review      1,104      2,836
    p50 latency                   10.1 s     16.6 s

  Half the money and half the calendar for a difference that no test can resolve.

THE SENTENCE FOR THE PAPER
  "The two reasoning settings were statistically indistinguishable on the held-out gold
   set (delta micro-F1 = 0.027, p = 0.08; MDE = 0.032 at n = 75) and produced identical
   label sets on 58 of 75 reviews. We selected the lower-cost setting, which also had
   the higher precision (0.887 vs 0.864) -- the property that matters when review-level
   labels are aggregated to game-level attributes."


====================================================================================================
3. WHY PROMPT v3 WAS REJECTED -- ALL CALCULATIONS
====================================================================================================
WHAT v3 IS AND WHAT IT WAS FOR
  v3 = v2 plus additional span requirements, rationales, and worked examples, added
  specifically to stop the model returning NONE and to make it commit to labels.
  It is a targeted intervention with a measurable objective: reduce the NONE rate.

EXACT SIZE COST
    measure                      v2        v3      delta
    prompt chars             96,265   119,790    +24.4%
    prompt lines                975     1,240    +27.2%
    file bytes               99,056   122,828
    measured input tok/review
      @ high                 25,831    30,402    +17.7%
      @ medium               25,552    30,123    +17.9%
      @ xhigh                27,072    33,163    +22.5%

  Cost consequence at 200k: v2 $407 vs v3 $414 at high; $839 vs $1,005 at xhigh.

DID IT DO WHAT IT WAS BUILT TO DO?  (tuning-50; gold NONE = 10/50, gold labels = 86)

    effort    v2: NONE  labels  microF1     v3: NONE  labels  microF1
    low         11/50      80    0.843        18/50      61    0.748
    medium      14/50      72    0.835        13/50      73    0.855
    high        11/50      82    0.845        13/50      74    0.850
    xhigh       11/50      88    0.851        10/50      90    0.818

  NO. At the setting we are shipping (high), v3 made it WORSE: NONE rose 11 -> 13 and
  total labels emitted FELL 82 -> 74. At low it is much worse (11 -> 18 NONE, labels
  80 -> 61). At medium and xhigh it is a wash. The intervention did not move its own
  target metric in the intended direction at any effort level.

QUALITY: PAIRED BOOTSTRAP v2 -> v3 AT MATCHED EFFORT (tuning-50, 20,000 resamples)

    effort       v2      v3    delta   P(v3 better)   identical label sets
    low       0.843   0.748   -0.095          0.069              32/50
    medium    0.835   0.855   +0.020          0.691              35/50
    high      0.845   0.850   +0.005          0.166              41/50
    xhigh     0.851   0.818   -0.032          0.488              39/50

  Read the P column: at high, P(v3 better) = 0.166 -- v2 is the more likely winner.
  v3 wins nothing decisively, loses decisively at low, and ties elsewhere. Across four
  efforts it has no consistent direction, which is itself the finding: a 24.4% larger
  prompt bought variance, not quality.

  Note also the stability difference across efforts:
    v2 spread over low/medium/high/xhigh:  0.835 - 0.851   (range 0.016)
    v3 spread over low/medium/high/xhigh:  0.748 - 0.855   (range 0.107)
  For a single non-repeatable 200k run, prefer the prompt whose quality does not depend
  on which effort knob it is paired with.

THE SENTENCE FOR THE PAPER
  "We tested an expanded prompt (+24.4% in length) that added span requirements,
   rationales and worked examples with the explicit aim of reducing abstention. It did
   not reduce the NONE rate at any reasoning effort (11 -> 13 of 50 at the selected
   setting), produced no significant change in micro-F1 (delta = +0.005, p = 0.83 at
   high), and was less stable across efforts (range 0.107 vs 0.016). We retained the
   shorter prompt."


====================================================================================================
4. IS CONSERVATIVE LABELLING A MODEL CHARACTERISTIC?
====================================================================================================
EVIDENCE FOR (three independent lines)
  1. It survives a prompt built to remove it. v3 added spans, rationales and examples
     aimed at exactly this behaviour and did not reduce the NONE rate (section 3).
  2. It is present in every model tested, not one:
       NONE rate on gold-75:  deepseek-v4-flash @ max  0.267
                              gpt-5.6-luna @ high      0.240
                              gpt-5.6-luna @ xhigh     0.173
                              human coders             0.067 - 0.120
     Three annotators from two providers all abstain more than any human.
  3. It is insensitive to the true base rate. Gold NONE is 20% on the tuning-50 and
     9.3% on the gold-75; the model returns NONE at 22% and 24% respectively. It is
     applying a roughly fixed internal threshold rather than calibrating to the data.

VERDICT ON THE CLAIM "AND IT IS GOOD"
  Half right, and the half that is right is the half that matters here.

  DEFENSIBLE:  the behaviour is a stable property of the annotator, not an artefact of
               our prompt, and conservative emission is the right bias for a design
               whose output is a game-level attribute built from thousands of reviews.
  NOT DEFENSIBLE AS WRITTEN: "under-labelling is good" full stop. It is good FOR THIS
               DESIGN. The same behaviour would be disqualifying for a review-level
               prevalence claim, for a per-review classifier benchmark, or for any
               statement of the form "X% of reviews contain no deceptive pattern".

  Write it as a property with a scope, not as a virtue:
    "The annotator is conservative: it abstains at roughly twice the human rate and
     this was not removable by prompt engineering. Because our unit of analysis is the
     game and each game contributes ~2,343 reviews, missed detections are recovered by
     aggregation, while the annotator's higher precision on emitted labels transfers
     directly to the game-level attribute. We report review-level prevalence as a lower
     bound and do not interpret the NONE class."


====================================================================================================
5. THE DESIGN CONSEQUENCE YOU MUST BUILD FOR
====================================================================================================
The precision-first argument is correct, but 0.887 precision over thousands of reviews
per game means absolute counts are dominated by noise. Scaling high's measured
false-positive rate to a median game of 2,343 reviews:

    label                        fp/75   rate/review   spurious per game
    S_Competition                    4        0.0533                 125
    T_PlayingByAppointment           2        0.0267                  62
    M_Gambling                       2        0.0267                  62
    M_PayToProgress                  1        0.0133                  31
    T_DailyRewards                   1        0.0133                  31
    T_Advertisement                  1        0.0133                  31
    T_InfiniteTreadmill              1        0.0133                  31
    TOTAL                           12        0.1600                 375
    (true positives, for scale)     94        1.2533               2,937

WHAT FOLLOWS
  - A rule of the form "game G has pattern P if any review mentions P" is useless:
    every game would carry almost every label. Do not use k = 1.
  - Game-level labels must be RATES (fraction of that game's reviews carrying P),
    compared across games, with a threshold set above the noise floor above.
  - The good news, and it is the real strength of this design: a false-positive rate
    that is roughly constant across games adds a roughly constant offset to every
    game's rate, and a constant under-detection rate scales every game's rate by
    roughly the same factor. Both largely CANCEL in between-game comparisons. The
    corpus is therefore strong for ranking and comparing games and weak for absolute
    per-review prevalence -- which is exactly the claim structure the paper should use.
  - The cancellation assumes the error rate does not correlate with game genre. That is
    worth one check once the corpus is labelled (e.g. whether M_Gambling precision
    differs on casino vs match-3 titles) before any cross-genre claim is made.
  - S_Competition is the one label to treat with suspicion: it is 4 of high's 12 false
    positives (33%) on gold-75 and the single largest noise source. Flag it in any
    per-label result.

CAVEAT ON THESE NUMBERS
  The false-positive rate is measured on gold-75, which is enriched for deceptive
  patterns (9.3% NONE against a random sample). The per-game noise figures above are
  therefore an upper bound in absolute terms; the ordering and the conclusion (use
  rates and a threshold, not counts) do not depend on the exact scaling.


====================================================================================================
6. WHAT THIS LICENSES, AND WHAT IT FORBIDS
====================================================================================================
LICENSED
  - Game-level pattern rates and comparisons/rankings between games.
  - Class-level (5-class) analyses, where inter-rater alpha is 0.665-0.815.
  - "The annotator agrees with human coders as well as human coders agree with each
    other" (MASI 0.518 for high, human range 0.511-0.658).
  - Co-occurrence structure between patterns at game level.

FORBIDDEN
  - "X% of reviews contain no deceptive pattern." The NONE class is the annotator's
    weakest decision (panel alpha 0.549) and only 33% of high's NONE calls on gold-75
    are correct.
  - Absolute review-level prevalence stated as a point estimate. Report as a lower
    bound with the gold-set calibration alongside.
  - Binary "this game has/does not have pattern P" from a single mention.
  - Any claim that xhigh or v3 is measurably better or worse. They are not.
  - Reporting the tuning-50 numbers as results. Selection set != test set; the tuning
    numbers appear in this file only to justify a design choice.
